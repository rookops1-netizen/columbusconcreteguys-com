Common Concrete Problems We Fix

We handle a wide range of concrete issues including cracks (hairline to structural), spalling and flaking surfaces, settling and sinking slabs, uneven joints, surface scaling, discoloration, and drainage problems. Each issue requires a specific repair approach. Hairline cracks may be sealed with epoxy injection, while larger structural cracks might require partial or full replacement. Sunken slabs can be lifted with mudjacking or polyurethane foam injection. We assess each situation individually and recommend the most cost-effective, long-lasting solution.

Our Repair Process

Every repair project begins with a thorough inspection to identify the cause of the damage. We evaluate the concrete condition, sub-base stability, drainage patterns, and any contributing factors. Based on our findings, we develop a repair plan that addresses both the visible damage and the underlying cause. Our repair techniques include epoxy crack injection, concrete resurfacing, slab jacking, partial replacement, and full replacement when necessary. We use high-quality repair materials that bond effectively with existing concrete and provide long-lasting results.

Concrete Resurfacing

For concrete surfaces that are worn, spalled, or stained but still structurally sound, resurfacing is an excellent option. We apply a thin overlay of polymer-modified concrete that bonds to the existing surface, creating a fresh, new appearance. Resurfacing can be finished with broom texture, stamped patterns, or exposed aggregate for a completely new look. This is often the most cost-effective solution for patios, walkways, and driveways that are showing their age but don't need full replacement.

When to Replace vs. Repair

Not all concrete problems can be repaired. We provide honest assessments about whether repair or replacement is the better option. Generally, if the concrete is structurally sound and the damage is cosmetic or localized, repair is the way to go. If the concrete is severely deteriorated, extensively cracked, or the sub-base has failed, replacement may be more cost-effective in the long run. We always explain your options and let you make the informed decision.

Understanding Why Concrete Fails in Ohio's Climate

Ohio's climate is particularly challenging for concrete. Freeze-thaw cycles — where water freezes and expands in concrete pores, then thaws and contracts — are the primary cause of concrete deterioration in Columbus. When water penetrates concrete and freezes, it expands by about 9%, creating internal pressure that causes cracking and spalling. Road salt and deicing chemicals accelerate this process by lowering the freezing point of water and increasing the number of freeze-thaw cycles. Poor drainage that allows water to pool on concrete surfaces exacerbates the problem. Understanding these failure mechanisms is key to implementing effective repairs that address the root cause, not just the symptoms.

Mudjacking vs. Polyurethane Foam Leveling

When concrete slabs settle or sink, two primary methods are used to lift them back into position. Mudjacking (also called slab jacking) involves pumping a cementitious slurry under the slab through small holes. The slurry fills voids and lifts the slab back to its original position. Mudjacking is cost-effective and has been used successfully for decades. Polyurethane foam leveling is a newer technique that injects expanding polyurethane foam under the slab. The foam expands to fill voids and lifts the slab with precise control. Polyurethane foam is lighter than mudjacking material, so it puts less stress on the soil below. It also cures faster — you can use the surface within 15 minutes instead of waiting 24-48 hours. We recommend the best method based on your specific situation.

Preventing Future Concrete Damage

After repairing your concrete, there are steps you can take to prevent future damage. Ensure proper drainage around all concrete surfaces — water should flow away from the concrete, not pool on it. Apply a quality concrete sealer every 2-3 years to reduce water penetration. In winter, use calcium magnesium acetate instead of sodium chloride (rock salt) for deicing — it's less damaging to concrete. Address cracks promptly when they appear — small cracks are much easier and less expensive to repair than large ones. Avoid using metal snow shovels on concrete surfaces. With proper care, your repaired concrete can last for many more years.

Frequently Asked Questions About Concrete Repair

Can cracked concrete be repaired?

Yes, most cracks in concrete can be repaired. Hairline cracks can be sealed with epoxy injection, while larger cracks may require more extensive repair. We assess each situation and recommend the best approach.

How much does concrete repair cost in Columbus?

How do you fix a sunken concrete slab?

Sunken slabs can be lifted using mudjacking (pumping a cementitious slurry underneath) or polyurethane foam injection. Both methods are effective, and we recommend the best approach based on your specific situation.
