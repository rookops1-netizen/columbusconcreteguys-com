Why Choose a Concrete Patio?

Concrete patios offer unmatched versatility, durability, and value. Unlike wood decking that requires annual staining and sealing, or paver patios that can shift and settle over time, a properly installed concrete patio provides a solid, permanent surface that requires minimal maintenance. Concrete can be finished in countless ways — stamped to mimic natural stone, exposed aggregate for texture, colored to match your home, or polished for a modern look. Concrete also stays cooler than pavers in summer heat and provides a smooth, level surface for furniture and foot traffic.

Patio vs. Deck: Which Is Right for You?

Many Columbus homeowners debate between a concrete patio and a wood or composite deck. Patios offer several advantages: they're more durable (lasting 25-30+ years vs. 10-15 for wood decks), require less maintenance (no staining, sealing, or replacing rotten boards), are more affordable per square foot, and stay cooler in summer heat. Patios also integrate better with landscaping and can be designed in virtually any shape. Decks are better for sloped properties where a patio would require extensive excavation, and they can be built at any height above ground level. For most Columbus properties with relatively flat backyards, a concrete patio offers the best combination of value, durability, and aesthetics.

Frequently Asked Questions About Concrete Patios

How long does a concrete patio last?

A properly installed concrete patio can last 25-30 years or more. Key factors include proper base preparation, correct concrete mix, adequate reinforcement, and regular sealing every 2-3 years.

How long does it take to build a concrete patio?

Most concrete patio projects take 3-5 days from excavation to finished surface. This includes site preparation, forming, pouring, finishing, and initial curing. The concrete continues to cure for 28 days before reaching full strength.

Can you add a patio to an existing concrete slab?

Yes, we can extend or resurface existing concrete patios. We assess the condition of the existing slab and recommend the best approach — whether that's adding an adjacent section, overlaying with stamped concrete, or complete replacement.
